## Context

I delivered a scoped Python engineering add-on for Dalian Ryobi. The work sat close to a customer's engineering workflow, so the useful outcome was not only code: the solution also needed a clear proposal, a workable operating path, understandable documentation, and a maintainable handoff.

This is a public-safe professional summary. It names the approved customer attribution but does not expose customer data, internal identifiers, proprietary implementation details, source code, or unsupported impact metrics.

## My ownership

I independently carried the work through the main delivery loop:

1. Investigated the technical problem and clarified the intended workflow.
2. Prepared and discussed a solution proposal.
3. Implemented the Python-based add-on and its surrounding workflow.
4. Answered technical questions and aligned the behavior with customer needs.
5. Wrote user or operation documentation for handoff.
6. Completed delivery and continued maintenance after handoff.

The ownership boundary is deliberately specific. It demonstrates end-to-end responsibility for a small customer project; it does not claim that I owned the customer's wider NX environment or every surrounding system.

## Engineering approach

The project required moving between investigation, implementation, validation, and communication rather than treating those as separate phases. I used Python as the delivery tool, kept the workflow understandable to its users, and treated documentation and maintenance as part of engineering quality.

The most important evidence is the continuity from technical question to delivered artifact: understand the need, propose a bounded solution, implement it, verify the handoff, and remain available for maintenance. No user counts, time savings, cost reductions, revenue, or performance figures are added because they are not part of the approved evidence.
